## Actionable Recommendations

### 1. **Complete Your Frameworks Before Moving On**
> Practice the full CIRCLES or similar framework until it's automatic. For product design: Context → Users → Insights → Prioritization → Solutions → MVP → Tradeoffs → Metrics. Don't let the interviewer cut you off mid-answer.

### 2. **Build a Metrics Hierarchy Muscle**
> For every practice question, force yourself to articulate: North Star (1), Primary metrics (2-3), Secondary metrics (3-4), and Guardrails (2-3). Explain *why* each matters.

### 3. **Add a Competitive Scan**
> Spend 30 seconds acknowledging competitors. For this question: "TripAdvisor does curated lists, Citymapper handles transit, but neither integrates real-time location with personalized recommendations—that's our advantage."

### 4. **Connect Features to Business Strategy**
> Every feature answer should touch on: How does this help Google? (Ad revenue? Ecosystem lock-in? Data advantage? Partner relationships?)

### 5. **Practice "Going Deeper" Prompts**
> When you give an initial answer, ask yourself: "What would a follow-up question be?" Then preemptively answer it. For metrics: "You might ask how we'd measure satisfaction—I'd propose a post-trip NPS survey triggered 24 hours after leaving the city."

### 6. **Quantify When Possible**
> Instead of "track adoption rate," say "I'd target 15% feature activation among detected tourists within Q1, growing to 30% by year-end based on similar feature launches."

### 7. **Mock Interview with Full Completion**
> Record yourself and ensure every answer has a clear beginning, middle, and end. Time yourself—a solid product design response should take 6-8 minutes, metrics 3-4 minutes.
