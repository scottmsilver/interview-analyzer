## 1. Summary of Current Interview Standards (2025/2026)

Based on my research, Google APM interviews evaluate candidates on **four core criteria**:

| Criteria | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Role-Related Knowledge** | Product sense, design thinking, metrics fluency, and technical understanding |
| **General Cognitive Ability** | Problem decomposition, structured thinking, analytical reasoning, and trade-off analysis |
| **Leadership** | Ability to drive outcomes, influence without authority, and take ownership |
| **Googleyness** | Comfort with ambiguity, collaborative nature, bias toward action, and cultural fit |

**Product Design Questions** are best approached using structured frameworks like the **CIRCLES Method**:
- **C**omprehend the situation
- **I**dentify the user
- **R**eport customer needs
- **C**ut through prioritization
- **L**ist solutions
- **E**valuate trade-offs
- **S**ummarize recommendation

**Metrics Questions** should demonstrate understanding of:
- North Star metrics vs. supporting metrics
- Leading vs. lagging indicators
- Potential trade-offs and guardrail metrics
- Connection between metrics and business/user outcomes

## 5. Specific, Actionable Recommendations

### For Product Design Questions:

1. **Complete the CIRCLES framework every time** — Practice finishing all seven steps even under time pressure. Set a personal rule: never end without summarizing your recommendation and key trade-offs.

2. **Differentiate from existing features** — Before proposing "Tourist Mode," acknowledge what Google Maps already offers (Explore tab, curated lists, Local Guides). Explain how your solution is *incrementally valuable*.

3. **Prioritize explicitly** — State: "If I had to build one thing first, it would be X because..." This demonstrates product judgment and execution mindset.

### For Metrics Questions:

4. **Use a metrics framework** — Structure responses as:
   - **North Star Metric** (1 primary measure of success)
   - **Supporting Metrics** (2-3 that indicate health)
   - **Guardrail Metrics** (1-2 that ensure you're not causing harm)
   
   Example: *"My North Star would be 'tourist itinerary completion rate' because it signals the feature delivered real value. Supporting metrics include adoption rate and repeat usage. Guardrail metrics would be standard Maps engagement to ensure we're not cannibalizing core use cases."*

5. **Always explain the 'why'** — For each metric, add one sentence on why it matters. This transforms a list into a demonstration of product thinking.

6. **Connect to business outcomes** — Google cares about impact. Tie metrics to strategic goals: *"Higher tourist engagement could increase Google Maps' positioning as the default travel companion, driving ad revenue in the travel/hospitality vertical."*

### General Interview Presence:

7. **Ask clarifying questions** — Before diving in, ask 1-2 questions to narrow scope (e.g., "Should I focus on a specific type of tourist—business travelers, families, solo backpackers?"). This shows maturity and prevents wasted effort on misaligned solutions.
