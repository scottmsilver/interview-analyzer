# Amazon PM / PM-T Interview Evaluation Rubric

## Core Philosophy & Bar-Raiser Expectations
Amazon evaluates candidates primarily through **Leadership Principles (LPs)** evidenced by **verifiable, recent, individual behavioral stories** (STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result) plus **Customer Obsession** and **Dive Deep** analytical rigor. Bar Raisers specifically probe past surface-level "we" phrasing to verify exact personal ownership, counterfactuals ("what would have happened if you hadn't acted?"), and quantified business/customer outcomes.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Leadership Principles & Ownership (40%)
- **Individual Agency ("I" vs "We")**: Clearly separates personal decisions, artifacts written, analyses run, and stakeholder negotiations from team context.
- **Customer Obsession**: Starts from customer pain and works backward (Working Backwards / PR-FAQ mindset), even when internal stakeholders push a tech-first or revenue-short-term path.
- **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit**: Demonstrates respectful, data-backed challenge to leadership or peers AND full commitment once a decision is made.
- **Deliver Results & Bias for Action**: Shows urgency under ambiguity, calculated risk-taking (two-way vs. one-way door decisions), and follow-through despite blockers.

### 2. Dive Deep & Analytical Precision (25%)
- **Quantified Impact**: Results include concrete baselines, deltas, timeframes, and denominators (e.g., "cut checkout drop-off from 18.4% to 13.1% over 6 weeks, adding $4.2M annualized margin" — not "improved conversion significantly").
- **Metric Hierarchy**: Connects input metrics (controllable levers like latency, defect rate, selection coverage) to output metrics (retention, free cash flow) with guardrail/counter-metrics (return rate, CS contact rate).

### 3. Product Design & Working Backwards (20%)
- Defines the target customer segment and core pain before proposing features.
- Evaluates operational scalability, fulfillment/supply constraints, unit economics, and edge cases.

### 4. Communication & STAR Structure (15%)
- **Crisp STAR Pacing**: Spends <=20% on Situation/Task, >=60% on personal Actions & trade-offs, and ~20% on quantified Results & learnings.
- Avoids rambling >150-word setup monologues; answers follow-up probes directly without getting defensive.

## Red Flags
- Persistent **"we" diffusion** after interviewer asks "what did *you* specifically do?"
- Unquantified or vague outcomes ("the team loved it", "NPS went up").
- Blaming teammates/management without owning personal learning or recovery actions.
- Proposing solutions that ignore operational/supply-side realities or unit economics.
