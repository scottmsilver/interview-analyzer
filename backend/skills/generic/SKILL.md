# General Interview Evaluation Rubric

## Core Philosophy & Bar-Raiser Expectations
Across product, technical, analytical, and leadership interviews, top candidates stand out by **framing problems before solving them**, **grounding claims in specifics and metrics**, **proactively naming trade-offs**, and **communicating with crisp structure and balanced dialogue**.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Problem Framing & Structure (25%)
- Clarifies goals, constraints, and definitions upfront before diving into answers.
- States an explicit roadmap/framework in the opening ~25% of the response and follows it cleanly.
- Segments users, stakeholders, or failure modes meaningfully before proposing solutions.

### 2. Depth, Specificity & Analytical Rigor (25%)
- Replaces vague generalities ("make it better", "track adoption/DAU/NPS") with concrete mechanisms, edge cases, and **metric hierarchies** (North Star + primary drivers + guardrail/counter-metrics).
- Considers ecosystem, operational, or multi-sided constraints (supply/demand, engineering cost, privacy/trust).

### 3. Trade-offs & Judgment (20%)
- Proactively identifies downsides, second-order effects, cannibalization, and risks without waiting for interviewer rescue prompts.
- Explains prioritization criteria and commits to clear decisions.

### 4. Ownership & Impact (15%)
- Uses clear **"I" ownership** for personal contributions vs. **"we"** for team context.
- Substantiates claims with quantified baselines, deltas, and timeframes.

### 5. Communication & Dialogue Calibration (15%)
- Maintains a conversational **~60:40 talk-to-listen ratio** with periodic check-ins rather than long unstructured monologues (>=120 words without signposting).
- Minimizes filler/hedging language and responds directly to interviewer probes.

## Red Flags
- Solutioneering without clarifying goals or segmenting users.
- Vanity/generic metrics without counter-metrics or operational definitions.
- Unchecked monologues, weak signposting, or hiding individual contribution behind "we".
- Missing critical blind spots until explicitly prompted/rescued by the interviewer.
