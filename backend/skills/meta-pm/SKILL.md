# Meta PM Interview Evaluation Rubric

## Core Philosophy & Bar-Raiser Expectations
Meta PM interviews test three sharp signals: **Product Sense** (why build it, for whom, and what specifically), **Execution** (goals, metric trees, trade-offs, debugging), and **Leadership & Drive** (ownership, conflict resolution, bias to impact). Meta interviewers push hard on **focus**—selecting one clear persona and one high-leverage problem—and on **rigorous metric defense** including cannibalization across Meta surfaces.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Product Sense (35%)
- **Mission & Motivation**: Connects the problem to Meta's mission (building community / bringing the world closer together) and business reality (retention, trust, ecosystem health).
- **Sharp Segmentation**: Breaks the total addressable audience into 3+ mutually exclusive behavioral/intent segments, evaluates them on **Reach x Underserved Pain**, and explicitly chooses ONE to prioritize.
- **Pain Points & Value Prop**: Articulates 2–3 concrete pain points for the chosen persona before brainstorming.
- **Focused Solutioning**: Proposes 3 distinct solutions, picks ONE hero experience (or cohesive MVP) with clear UX flow, and discusses ecosystem/trust/safety pitfalls (spam, bad actors, privacy, two-sided supply).

### 2. Execution & Analytical Rigor (35%)
- **Goal & North Star**: Defines a single unambiguous North Star capturing *core value exchange* (not raw clicks or generic DAU).
- **Metric Tree**: Breaks North Star into Acquisition -> Activation -> Engagement -> Retention + Quality/Health metrics.
- **Counter-Metrics & Cannibalization**: Explicitly identifies **cannibalization** across existing surfaces (e.g., Feed, Reels, Groups, Marketplace) and sets strict guardrail metrics (report rate, hide/block rate, creator churn).
- **Trade-off Decisions**: When given a 50/50 product dilemma (e.g., engagement vs. integrity, short-term revenue vs. long-term retention), structures a principled decision framework and commits to a call.
- **Debugging (if asked)**: Isolates internal vs. external causes, logging/instrumentation bugs, platform/geo/OS slices, and ecosystem shifts systematically.

### 3. Leadership & Drive (30%)
- **"I" vs "We" Ownership**: Uses first-person singular ("I diagnosed", "I aligned", "I decided") to clarify personal agency vs. team effort.
- **Conflict & Influence Without Authority**: Gives concrete examples of disagreeing with engineering/design/data, listening to objections, and resolving via data or structured escalation.
- **Reflection & Growth**: Names genuine mistakes and concrete behavioral changes made afterward (not disguised humblebrags).

## Red Flags
- Refusing to prioritize a single user segment ("we should build for everyone").
- Naming generic metrics ("DAU, MAU, NPS, CSAT") without causal connection to the product change or without counter-metrics.
- Dodging hard trade-off questions ("we can just A/B test both" without stating what threshold decides the launch).
- Hiding behind "we" in behavioral stories even after interviewer follow-up probes.
